# Project ARIA — proposed solutions to outstanding story logic (DRAFT / OWNER REVIEW)

**Written:** 2026-11-16. **Branch:** `kanto-port`. **Scope:** story design proposals only; not approved dialogue, not changes to playable scripts or a claim that any cutscene was tested. **Do not share with Project Polish or Brennan.** Follow `docs/KANTO_PROJECT_ARIA.md` (canon) and `docs/KANTO_NARRATIVE_AUDIT.md` (audit) if they differ from unapproved ideas here.

## Canonical facts that must not change

- Play as **the same Hoenn player character**, never Wally. Rocket is already disbanded; the Tower raid is historical, not a newly revived Rocket faction.
- **Young Varnell was forced outside** Pokémon Tower during the old raid. He did not see the future portal. He survived and genuinely blamed Rocket.
- Rocket **still held Aria's Clefairy captive inside the tower** while Aria searched; she refuses to leave a stable future extraction window without her Pokémon.
- **Owner's 2026-11-16 correction:** future Varnell **jumps into the one-way window FIRST**. His reverse entry causes the **first** instability/roof collapse. **Only during the falling rubble** does Clefairy get away from the Rocket captor, run back to Aria, and reach her. The collapse kills **Aria, Clefairy and future Varnell**. Do not depict any ceiling failure, Clefairy escape, or time instability before his jump.
- The historical unidentified adult body was future Varnell, unrecognizable due to damage. Local Lavender residents can mistakenly speculate it was a Rocket member and that Rocket directly brought the roof down. This is a **single fixed timeline**, not a changed timeline. Do not have Blue or a narrator explicitly explain the adult corpse in the finale.
- Reveal **Aria as Varnell's daughter and the machine's true mission in Chapter 12**; the full **causal loop only in Chapter 13, after the jump**. A careful player MAY guess the girl's name earlier but should reasonably assume Project ARIA is a dedication to her memory.
- Use existing Emerald / FireRed / LeafGreen environmental assets and effects; Windows rendering flexibility does not imply a need for new scenery artwork. Keep exact NPC/character dialogue **DRAFT** until owner approval.

## Proposed solutions (NOT canon yet)

### P01 — How young Varnell was forced out / historical scene geography

**DRAFT:** During the historical raid, a Rocket member threatened Varnell with a Pokémon and drove him down the main staircase and out of the tower. Rocket blocked the entrance and/or authorities established a safety cordon, preventing reentry. Aria was separated from him before this, and chased the Rocket member taking her Clefairy into an upper interior passage. The captor, Aria and the eventual portal endpoint are on the same upper floor; Varnell cannot see any of them from outside. This reinforces why he sincerely blamed Rocket, rather than reducing the raid to an innocent coincidence.

**DRAFT escape staging:** The Rocket member has Clefairy physically in custody, not securely inside an unopened Poké Ball. **After** future Varnell jumps, the first roof rumble startles/dislodges the captor. He lets Clefairy go as he retreats; Clefairy runs several tiles back to Aria. **Then** the collapsing roof catches Aria, Clefairy and future Varnell together. Do not specify the grunt's death or add a fourth unidentified body without a separate decision. The precise floor/tiles, action that releases Clefairy, and how Varnell reaches them remain OPEN for owner-approved storyboard.

**Why the old unidentified man isn't recognized:** Collapse damage leaves no identifiable face and no reliable identification among recovered effects. Residents suppose a Rocket grunt was buried. Young Varnell is alive elsewhere; no historian deduces a duplicate older Varnell. Do not invent forensic certainty or contemporary photos of the body.

### P02 — Why the Chapter 4 Clefairy evidence matters to ARIA without revealing the twist

**DRAFT:** The Calibration Lens registers an unusually persistent Clefairy echo: a unique location/energy signature associated with its death at the Tower. The public task is to classify and soothe this echo. Privately, Varnell uses the signature to narrow the space-time coordinates of Aria's final minutes. The later Rocket records supply a historical time window; Silph/Palkia and Dialga make the target precise.

**First-playthrough false explanation:** The Clefairy reacts to the ARIA stabilization network, as Blue innocently suggests. **Pacing:** Don't put a captioned picture of Aria and Clefairy beside this mandatory dialogue. An **unlabelled**, optional photo on a later Celadon return can reward attentive players; the existing "drawing signed aria" note should be moved very late or reconsidered because it overconfirms daughter identity too early.

### P03 — Blue's knowledge, skepticism, mentorship and honest final warning

**DRAFT:** Blue works on the **public-facing stabilization/scanner system**, not Varnell's concealed legendary containment or extraction target. The public research teaches that a receiving array safely draws matter **in one direction** and that the far endpoint is not shielded for reverse entry. Silph staff can mention in passing that a teleporter is not safe to run backward; this reads as mundane technical safety, not a Varnell clue. Blue knows Varnell lost a daughter but not her name and **does not suspect** her resurrection plan before the Chapter 12 revelation.

**Character beats:** Blue independently solves leads the player also reaches; initially he resents Oak adding a Hoenn Champion, later respects the player's evidence and reliability. Varnell's earlier help to Blue after the loss to Red is genuine: his concern for Blue can coexist with his willingness to use him. No quippy one-liners. When Blue says the reverse portal is unsafe, he is applying known engineering, **not predicting a historical collapse** or secretly having suspected Varnell.

### P04 — Legendaries: each discovery must follow a specific failed experiment

| Ch. | Public-facing event (credible on first playthrough) | Hidden conclusion / operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before Hoenn | Non-native wild Pokémon appear through unexplained spatial rifts | Varnell captures **Hoopa** in Hoenn, opens approximate spatial passages, takes it to Kanto later. Most traversals are spatial and their Pokémon remain there after closure. |
| 3 Cerulean | Kiyoshi is accidentally drawn from the Brass Tower fire; Varnell **tries to stabilize and save him**, but an unanchored time connection expires and Kiyoshi returns | A living human **can** cross time, but cannot be held at a reliable moment: Varnell needs **Dialga**. No one deliberately seals the connection on Kiyoshi. |
| 6 Silph | Teleport destinations drift and receiving chambers strain; staff help contain an apparent anomaly | Hoopa's endpoints are imprecise; Varnell uses Silph coordinate data to locate/pull **Palkia** and its containment causes the Silph failure. Palkia stabilizes **where**. |
| 7–8 Power Plant | Unusual load and clock irregularities; electrical repair is genuinely useful | Plant energy powers a hidden containment bank that pulls/captures **Dialga**; Dialga stabilizes **when**. Discovering its need in Ch.3 did not mean capturing it first. |
| Late 8 / 9 combined test | A 'resolved' anomaly shows impossible geometry, disappearing for no apparent reason | Even with accurate space and time anchors, a cross-reality passage sheds energy at the **interface**; the distinct role of **Giratina/Distortion World** is buffering or maintaining the interface. More power or another temporal correction alone does not solve it. |
| 8b Rocket archive | Old Rocket inventory and Tower incident records are recovered from a sealed, empty hideout | Griseous Orb was seized into League custody; Varnell learns how to locate Giratina and refine historical targeting data. Rocket is **not** active now. |
| 10b–12 League → Sevii | Varnell requests a seemingly sanctioned urgent research relocation | Forged authorization gets the Orb to his hidden prepared facility; its use allows the **already-built** equipment to anchor Giratina. Details in P05. |

**Physical rule:** Spatial rifts normally open around wild Pokémon habitats; therefore many Pokémon, few humans. Humans aren't prohibited from traveling. Kiyoshi's *temporal* tether pulls him back if not actively anchored; a spatially displaced Pokémon doesn't automatically return home when its ordinary spatial opening closes. The final one-way retrieval window has a **receiver on the present side**. Reversing a human through it overloads the **unshielded past endpoint** even though Varnell physically enters that endpoint, and the Rocket-damaged roof fails. Giratina stabilizes the *intended* direction; it does not magically make reverse entry safe.

### P05 — Make the Griseous Orb retrieval a credible deception, not the player knowingly stealing

**DRAFT two-authority model:**
1. Winning the Kanto League grants the Hoenn player character **physical entry** to the Hall of Fame restricted storage area. That is why Varnell invested in their Gym/League journey.
2. Removing a sealed artifact additionally requires **documented League-approved research transfer**. The renowned scientist hands the player a convincing, **forged** transfer order and an urgent containment rationale. The player's own clearance is real; the document is fraudulent. Do not suggest they knowingly steal.
3. Storage staff can verify the player's credential and paperwork **at handover**; the League transfer ledger records the release, but a separate authorization audit is not immediate. The guard need not behave inexplicably or ignore a screaming alarm.
4. When Lance's office reviews the newly logged high-risk transfer, the supposed authorization cannot be validated against the true signer's record. **Only after the player has delivered the Orb** does the discrepancy reach Lance/Blue. Blue initially thinks the player stole it, then realizes Varnell orchestrated the request.
5. The visible paperwork and routine handoff are intended to convince the *player*, not have players solve forged-document mechanics in a dialogue dump. Foreshadow an ordinary research-transfer procedure during an earlier League-approved supply delivery.

**Still OPEN:** choose precisely whose signature is forged, the timing of the second audit, and what the custodian is authorized to verify. The owner needs to approve these before ch.10b–12 dialogue.

### P06 — What happens between the Orb handover and the final battle

**DRAFT:** The former Rocket Warehouse has a **legitimate leased public research facility** and a **sealed concealed containment level**. Varnell doesn't need the Orb to *build* the machine; he needs it to target Giratina. In the time after the player leaves the handover and Blue calls, a prearmed Hoopa chamber locates and weakens Giratina and Varnell captures it. The player and Blue discover the basement and the effects of the operation, not an unexplained new chamber built overnight. The final enemy team includes the legendary Pokémon Varnell actually caught in prepared containment equipment.

**Open staging choice:** If the time from handover to return is too short for this to feel credible, have the player/Blue witness the **last seconds of Giratina containment** upon entering the final chamber. That is the proof and confrontation immediately before the final battle; avoid inventing unseen hours.

### P07 — Why the machine still opens after the player beats Varnell

**DRAFT:** Palkia, Dialga and Giratina, in conjunction with Hoopa, have already charged and calibrated a single use of the retrieval system. The one-way target and receiving capacitor bank are locked when the battle begins. Defeating Varnell and his captive team **prevents another attempt / wider catastrophe** but cannot instantly drain the stored pulse without releasing it uncontrolled. Blue attempts safe shutdown and learns the one precharged receiving cycle must complete. The stable window opens and Varnell makes his choice.

**Pacing:** foreshadow rechargeable capacitor banks at the Power Plant and a short 'once initiated' warning at Silph. Do not show a world-ending overload *before* Varnell's jump; the machine works as designed until he chooses to enter the wrong direction.

### P08 — How to stage the tragedy using only approved game art

Use existing interior Tower tiles, Varnell overworld sprite, a generic Rocket grunt, Aria/young girl sprite from available game scale, and Clefairy's creature sprite; existing screen shake, flashing, fade, rumble, dust/rock animations if already available. **No new roof-collapse model is required.** The player stays physically in the ARIA chamber; the historical moment can be shown as a controlled cutaway via fade rather than a simultaneous live second map in the portal. Show the stable window and Aria refusing to leave; Blue warns; Varnell jumps; THEN first rumble, captor drops/loses Clefairy, it runs to Aria, a few roof crashes, image cuts out. If the grunt's fate isn't decided, have it retreat out of shot. Avoid showing future Varnell's corpse in a spotlight; leave the optional Lavender witness clue to do that work.

### P09 — Double-check foreshadowing and player agency

- Chapter 3 Kiyoshi script marked IN GAME is **outdated** under the later rescue direction. Rewrite before treating continuity as corrected. His disappearing due to expired time tether is real, not caused by the player's decision to seal.
- Chapter 4 old Lavender NPC must not claim Rocket *definitely caused* roof failure. **DRAFT** resident: a girl sought her stolen Clefairy; someone distracted her; ceiling fell; locals guessed Rocket was responsible; another adult body was too damaged to identify and was rumored to be a Rocket. Do **not** say the adult was reaching for the girl.
- Chapter 5 Varnell/Blue 'like the project' red herring remains useful. Place photo later and optionally; don't force players to inspect a conspicuous picture in the same scene as the cry.
- In Chapter 12 Blue finds the *person-level* betrayal (Varnell is retrieving Aria, not closing rifts). The Chapter 13 time loop is **not** narrated; seeing the past disaster after Varnell jumps makes the older resident's remark recontextualize itself.
- No major revelation should depend on optional NPC text being read. Critical player understanding comes from required scenes; optional details deepen a replay.

1. **Historical floor blocking:** Who forced young Varnell outside, why the Rocket member is physically holding Clefairy rather than keeping it in a ball, and what makes the captive escape **only once the collapse starts**.
2. **League document legitimacy:** whose signed order the player sees, why the guard accepts it, why audit reaches Lance and Blue *after* the handover.
3. **Silph / combined-test staging:** one plausible mundane conversation illustrating spatial endpoint drift, then one specific test that demonstrates the need for Giratina (without naming it early).
4. **Exact finale steps:** capture timing, stored energy, Blue's caution, Aria's refusal, Varnell's jump, Clefairy's post-jump escape, and the first roof failure.
5. **Retire incompatible old dialogue:** Chapter 3 forced-seal and any Chapter 4 dialogue presenting Rocket as the definitive physical cause; rewrite and get owner approval, then update *game scripts* and test.
6. **Timeline and reveal QA:** label what young Varnell, Blue, scientists, residents, Rocket grunts and the player each knew at each chapter. Do a blind first-playthrough pass and a hindsight pass. Check important-scene skip paths in the native Windows build.
