# Kanto: Project ARIA — design doc

Status: **design, owner-approved decisions recorded 2026-11-13.** Nothing here is built yet.
Read this before touching anything Kanto. Owner rules from `WALLY_JOURNEY_OWNER_REQUIREMENTS.md` still apply
(show pictures before visual/wording changes, no guessing, GBA saves must import).

Goal from the owner: **a far more narrative story than Hoenn had.** Every chapter should have a scene,
a reveal or a choice, not just a gym.

---

## 1. Locked decisions

| Topic | Decision |

## 2. Aria and Varnell (the emotional core)

- Aria died years ago, **caught in a Rocket attack** — wrong place, wrong time. Random and senseless,
  which is exactly why Varnell can't accept it. **Decided 2026-11-13 (revised): in Pokémon Tower,
  Lavender, during Team Rocket's raid in the original FRLG story.** Varnell was researching the tower's
  unusual readings with Aria along. Rocket stole her Clefairy; she chased after it; both died in the tower.
  This mirrors FRLG's own Marowak tragedy in the same raid. Mr. Fuji was there.
- **Rule for every clue: it only makes sense looking back.** Nothing may give the twist away early.
- At Lavender (ch. 4) Varnell won't enter Pokémon Tower and sends the player alone ("the readings need a
  trainer's Pokémon"). In the moment it's an odd excuse; after the reveal it's a father who can't walk
  back into the place she died.
- **The real purpose of the Lavender assignment (owner, 2026-11-13):** the Clefairy's spirit has stayed in
  the tower. Varnell secretly wants to know whether, if *it* lingered, something of Aria might still be
  there and reachable. The player thinks they're calibrating a lens to study the tears; really they're
  his test of whether the dead leave a trace the rift can reach. The player doesn't know this.
- **Report-back scene (owner, 2026-11-13):** Blue is there when the player reports to Varnell. Blue (who
  never knew her name) mentions it in passing:
  > Blue: "It was weird. It kept saying 'Aria.' Like the project. Maybe the tears pulled it in like the
  > other Pokémon that shouldn't be here... and it's reacting to the machine somehow?"
  > Varnell: "......"
  > Varnell: "...Possibly. The tower's readings were high. Echoes tend to latch onto strong signals.
  > Good work, both of you."
  Blue honestly thinks the ghost means *the project* (he never knew her name). Varnell lies by agreeing —
  he uses Blue's mistake as cover. This doubles as a red herring that points players at the machine, not a person.
  Exactly one beat of silence, then he recovers. Longer or emotional reactions would tip off sharp players.
- For most of the game Varnell seems kind, brilliant and genuinely helpful. **That's a mask.**
- Underneath he's twisted: he knows folding space-time could destroy the world and **doesn't care.**
  A world without Aria isn't a world worth being in, so the risk was worth it. No remorse, no redemption.
- At the revelation Blue asks: you could have destroyed everyone, cost billions of lives. Varnell's answer
  is that a world without his daughter isn't worth having. All he cares about is a world where she's with him.
- ARIA is built to fold space and time open far enough to pull Aria back from the moment before she died.
  The "displacements" across Kanto are its test runs leaking.

### How the name hides in plain sight
"Project ARIA" is said all game and sounds like any acronym. Clues the player can miss:
- A child's drawing in his office signed "aria" in lowercase — a girl holding a **Clefairy** (pays off the Lavender ghost).
- Staff: "The Director won't let anyone else touch ARIA's settings."
- The holding bay terminal: "Good night, Aria."
- Mr. Fuji (who was in the tower during the Rocket raid) mentions a grieving father who visits a grave
  "with no Pokémon name on it."

**Mid-game photo + the Clefairy (owner, 2026-11-13):** the player finds an unlabelled photo of a little
girl holding a Clefairy. In Lavender Tower the Clefairy echo calls out "Aria". Only players who put the
photo and the Clefairy together realise Aria is his daughter — most won't.
**Built-in misdirection:** even players who do will assume he simply named the project after her out of
love, to honour her while fixing the anomalies. Nothing tells them he's trying to *bring her back*. Their
"solved it" guess hides the real twist.

**Reveal:** the machine exists to bring her back, and he never cared what it cost. For players who missed
the clues, it's also the moment they learn Aria was his daughter and the project carried her name all along.

## 2a. The anomalies and the timeline (owner, 2026-11-13)

- **In-story reason for every region's Pokémon living in Hoenn and Kanto:** "the anomalies" — Pokémon from
  other regions appearing where they shouldn't. This is the canon explanation for the game's wild Pokémon.
- Kanto follows the same Poké Ball-only rule as Hoenn.
- Varnell is a **world-renowned** researcher; his base is in Kanto.
- **Timeline:**
  1. **Before the player moves to Littleroot**, Varnell caught **Hoopa in Hoenn** (Hoopa is tied to Hoenn
     in ORAS canon) and began experimenting there. Hoopa's rings opening is why other regions' Pokémon are
     already in Hoenn when the game starts. Nobody knows Hoopa is the cause.
  2. The Groudon/Kyogre/Rayquaza crisis happens later, during the player's journey — not his doing. He
     watches the player handle it.
  3. Realising he needs more pieces, he takes Hoopa back to his Kanto lab. The rings follow Hoopa, so the
     anomalies seem to "spread" to Kanto — that's why Oak calls the player now.
  4. His theory builds piece by piece: **Palkia** to control space, **Dialga** to control time, and finally
     **Giratina** — the missing piece (antimatter / the Distortion World). With space, time and antimatter
     he believes Hoopa's portal can bring Aria back into this world. That machine is ARIA.
- **Captures are story beats during Kanto (owner, 2026-11-13).** The player never knows these are
  legendaries, or that Hoopa, Palkia, Dialga or Giratina are in Kanto. Each capture causes something in
  the story, or something the player does helps it happen. Method: **Hoopa's portals pull the legendary
  into a containment chamber; machines wear it down until he can catch it.** He isn't a strong enough
  trainer to beat them normally, so he weakens them first — but he **does** capture them, and uses them
  in his final battle. That's the holding bay.
- Draft mapping (owner to adjust):

| Capture | What the player unknowingly does | What everyone sees | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palkia (space) | Each tear "closed" with Varnell's device gives him a lock on where space is weakest — how the portal finds Palkia | The Silph malfunction: the building warps, Saffron quarantined. Really the wear-down chamber straining to hold Palkia | 2–6 |
| Dialga (time) | Varnell sends the player to restart the Power Plant "to stabilise readings"; the power feeds ARIA | Time slips: clocks stop in Fuchsia, Mansion diaries look freshly written, the displaced person from another time | 7–8 |
| Giratina (final piece) | After the 8th badge Varnell privately asks for an item in the **Hall of Fame vault**; the player beats Lance, takes the **Griseous Orb** and hands it over — it lets him pin down Giratina | His panicked PokéNav call, the handover, then the capture and the reveal | 10b–12 |

## 2b. Blue and Varnell

- After losing to Red, Blue lost all his confidence. **Varnell gave him a second chance**: a research spot
  in his lab, helping him rebuild. Blue owes him, and loves him like a mentor.
- Blue knows only that Varnell lost a daughter to Team Rocket years ago. Varnell won't talk about it;
  it hurts too much. **Blue never knew her name.**
- Blue was already investigating the anomalies with Oak and Varnell when they invited the player from
  Hoenn. He doesn't understand why — he's capable, he was already on it, and these are *his* people.
  So he's cold, unimpressed and irritated with the player. He has his own personality; he's not a jerk.
- Early: Blue investigates on his own. You cross paths because you're chasing the same thing; he shares
  nothing. Over time he starts working with you.
- **Varnell uses Blue too, and doesn't mind.** Blue's investigation data feeds ARIA. Bringing in the player
  is purely practical: he needs a Kanto badge holder's clearance, and Blue knows him and the lab too well
  — Blue would notice sooner. A stranger from Hoenn won't.
- Saffron: Blue recognises ARIA markings from his time in the lab and starts to doubt the man who saved him.
- The reveal hits Blue harder than the player: the man who gave him a second chance was using him all along. Viridian is Blue choosing to stand with the player against
  his mentor — the turning point of his arc, not just the 8th badge.

## 2c. The gym challenge is clearance (the player's progress builds ARIA)

- After the Rocket years the Kanto League rebuilt security: Kanto badges carry League clearance, and
  restricted sites (tear zones, Silph's sealed floors, Seafoam station, Cinnabar's lab) open only for
  licensed Kanto trainers. Hoenn badges don't count.
- Wally's send-off is why the player takes the challenge; **Varnell sponsors it** (registration, trainer
  pass) and cheers every badge.
- Each badge opens a restricted site; each assignment sends the player into one.
- Twist: Varnell needed a badge holder's clearance. Beating Lance is the last one and opens the
  **Indigo Plateau's sealed vault.** The player's own wins built the machine.
- As a registered challenger, beating Lance is a real Kanto win — which is why Kanto challengers later
  come to the player's title defenses in Hoenn.

## 5. Levels and EXP (simulated — see `tools/exp_curve_full.py`, `tools/exp_curve_kanto.py`)

- **Normal EXP. No Kanto EXP cut.** Gen 7 scaled EXP already slows over-levelled teams.
- A player who beats every Hoenn trainer reaches Champion Wally at about **55** (top ~58);
  with the post-game one-time fights, about **60**.
- **Route trainers:** fixed levels per area, about **5 below** where the simulation says the player is
  (the same gap Hoenn has at around level 20–29).
- **Wild Pokémon:** fixed, about **4 below** the local trainers. Not scaled.
- **Gyms, Elite Four, Varnell:** scaled like Hoenn's bosses (`wj_trainer_scaling.h`: minimum + margin over
  your highest, locked at first fight).

| Fight | Player avg (sim) | Boss ace minimum | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gyms 1–2 | 58–62 | 64 | +2 |
| Gyms 3–4 | 62–66 | 68 | +2 |
| Gyms 5–6 | 66–70 | 72 | +2 |
| Gyms 7–8 (Blue) | 70–73 | 76 | +3 |
| Kanto E4 | 73–76 | 78–80 | +3 |
| Varnell | 76–78 | 80–82 | +3–4 |

- Leaders are veteran versions: six Pokémon, fully evolved, newer species that fit their type
  (e.g., Brock: Rhyperior, Tyranitar, Aerodactyl, Gigalith, Kabutops, Golem — **no Geodude**).
- Re-run the simulator on the real Kanto trainer data once it exists; adjust until each boss is 1–3 above.

## 6. Champion defenses (moved after Kanto)

- The title-defense ladder starts **when the player returns from Kanto**, not right after Hall of Fame.
  Story: you come home to defend your title now that everything is done.
- Keep every existing Hoenn challenger (Treston, Wally, Wallace, Steven, rival, Gym Leaders…).
- Add Kanto challengers: the Kanto Gym Leaders, Blue, the Kanto Elite Four.
- **Red** is the final challenger, at the top of the curve (current defenses run 74–90).
- Needs a code change: today the ladder triggers on repeat E4 runs after the Hall of Fame.

## 7. Assets (owner rules)

- **Only FireRed and Emerald tiles and sprites.** No hand-drawn art. Outside assets only if free to use,
  credited, tested in game, and shown to the owner first.
- **Recolours are allowed** as new Varnell-only tileset copies, so no other map changes.
- One map = one tileset pair. Different rooms can use different sets (like Silph Co.).
- Mockups (made from real tiles, renderer not in-game): Varnell Institute public floor (Cinnabar Lab set),
  ARIA core (Aqua Hideout facility set), holding bay with four sealed tanks, recoloured "Void" (calm) and
  "Red Alert" (ARIA firing) — palette swap is a live story trigger, plus a breathing pulse on the tanks.
- Varnell's sprite: an existing scientist/gentleman sprite with a new palette unless a free outside sprite
  is approved.

## 8b. More owner decisions (2026-11-13)

- **Music:** normal FireRed/LeafGreen music. A Varnell theme later.
- **Varnell's look:** on hold.
- **Hoopa:** nobody knows Hoopa is causing the displacements until the reveal. Its rings are the hidden
  cause of the "anomalies". No cold open that shows it.
- **Travel:** Kanto opens after the first Champion Wally win. The player **can** go back to Hoenn, but
  everything that would break the level curve — Champion defenses and the rest of that ladder — stays
  locked until Kanto's story is beaten. (Gym Leader rematch tier 3, the final rival and Steven are in the
  simulated pre-Kanto curve already; owner to confirm whether they stay open.)
- **Research item: Pokémon Crossroads-style travel.** Owner wants to look into flying between regions with
  the overworld visible. Crossroads is a GBA Emerald hack on pokeemerald-expansion, built on StrangeQuark94's
  multi-region pokeemerald fork. Its public thread describes region flags, region-aware Fly and seamless
  region transitions; it doesn't say the source is public. To do: find out exactly how its travel looks and
  works (and whether code is available and allowed) before designing ours.

## 8b2. Why the League has the Griseous Orb

Team Rocket stole relics around the world, the Griseous Orb among them. When Rocket fell, the League
seized everything and sealed it in the **Hall of Fame vault** — too dangerous to return or display.
Varnell learns this from Rocket's inventory, which the player retrieves for him in the Rocket Hideout (8b).

## 8c. Phone calls

- The **PokéNav works in Kanto** (Match Call). Blue, Varnell and Oak call the player during the story.
- **Wally calls** now and then with updates on holding the player's Champion spot while they investigate.
